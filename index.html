Pricing meters — expanded

Each vendor's primary meter explained in plain English, plus what the buyer actually gets on their invoice.

Hightouch — Modular + usage-based

In simple terms: You buy only the pieces you want (like picking items off a menu). Each piece has its own price.

  • Platform fee — a fixed monthly cost just to have access to the product.
  • Active syncs — you pay based on how many sync jobs are actively running (e.g., "push segment X to Salesforce every hour"). More active syncs = higher bill.
  • Modules — add-ons like Identity Resolution, Real-Time Personalization, Match Booster, or Customer Studio. Each one adds to the bill.

Example: A company pays a $30K platform fee + $20K for 50 active syncs + $40K for Identity Resolution module = $90K/year. No charge for storing data.

Census — Destinations, syncs, and seats

In simple terms: Three dials: how many systems you send data to, how often you sync, and how many team members log in.

  • Destinations — each downstream tool you connect (Salesforce, Marketo, Facebook Ads, etc.) counts as one. Tiers include a certain number; beyond that, you pay per extra destination.
  • Sync frequency / volume — higher plans unlock more frequent syncs and higher row limits.
  • Seats — each person who uses Census counts as a seat.
  • No data storage charge — data stays in your warehouse; Census just activates it.

Example: Growth plan at ~$14K/year might include 10 destinations and 5 seats. Adding an 11th destination or a 6th seat pushes you into the next tier.

RudderStack — Pay per event

In simple terms: One meter. Count every event (page view, click, purchase) that flows through the platform. That's your bill.

  • Events — each tracked action (page_view, button_click, checkout, etc.) is one event. You pay per million events per month.
  • No MTU trap — unlike Segment, RudderStack doesn't charge for "Monthly Tracked Users" where one user with 100 events counts the same as one with 10,000. You pay for actual volume.
  • Linear and predictable — 10M events costs 2× what 5M events costs. No cliffs.

Example: A mid-market e-commerce site doing 50M events/month might pay ~$2–3K/month, scaling linearly as traffic grows.

GrowthLoop — Unique customer records + modules

In simple terms: Count how many unique people are in your database. Pick the plan that matches. Add modules if you need them.

  • Unique records — each distinct person (customer, lead, prospect) counts once, regardless of how many events they have.
  • Three public tiers:
    • Basic — up to 1 million records
    • Growth — up to 10 million records
    • Enterprise — 10 million+ records
  • Modules — journey orchestration, AI audiences, activation channels are add-ons.
  • No storage fee — GrowthLoop runs on top of your data cloud; compute happens there.

Example: A company with 7M customers lands in Growth tier. They pay one predictable fee whether each customer has 10 events or 10,000.

ActionIQ — Quote-based enterprise

In simple terms: No public prices. Every deal is custom-negotiated. Usually six- or seven-figure annual contracts.

  • Custom contract — pricing factors in audience size, number of departments using the tool, feature set, and support tier.
  • HybridCompute architecture — queries run inside the customer's warehouse (zero-copy), so vendor isn't charging for storage.
  • No self-serve signup — you talk to sales, get a demo, then a quote.

Example: A Fortune 500 retailer might sign a $500K–$2M/year deal covering unlimited users, full module suite, and dedicated support.

Treasure AI — P+B Units

In simple terms: You pay based on two things TD stores in its warehouse: how many customer profiles you have, and how many behavioral events are attached to them. Both count.

  • 1 P+B Unit equals either 1 million profiles OR 1 billion behavioral events. Whichever you consume, you pay for.
  • Known profiles (identified customers with email/phone/ID) count 1:1 — one known profile = 1 profile toward your unit total.
  • Unknown profiles (anonymous visitors) count at a 20:1 ratio — 20 anonymous profiles = 1 profile toward your unit total.
  • Billing rule: TD looks at your daily profile/behavior count across the month, throws out the top 3 days (to avoid penalizing spikes), and bills based on the 4th-highest day.
  • Both dimensions add up — if you have 5M profiles (= 5 units) and 3B behaviors (= 3 units), you consume 8 P+B Units total.

Example: A retailer with 10M known customers and 15B purchase/browse events consumes 25 P+B Units. The more behavioral history you enrich profiles with, the higher the bill — even if you haven't activated any of it yet.

How Treasure AI differs

  • Unique meter. Everyone else prices the work done (events, syncs, activations, destinations). TD prices the resident inventory — profiles and behavioral events.
  • Dual axis. A customer row and their associated behaviors are both counted. Competitors use a single dimension.
  • Data-resident, not data-activated. In composable models, history in Snowflake/BigQuery isn't penalized — only flow-through is. TD charges for the warehouse footprint itself.
Bottom line: Composable CDPs meter activation (value created). TD meters retention (data stored + enriched).

Effectiveness (alignment with customer-perceived value)

  • Strong RudderStack, Hightouch — cost tied to activation work (events delivered, syncs run).
  • Strong GrowthLoop — tied to audience scale, a metric marketers natively understand.
  • Mixed Treasure AI — economically defensible but decoupled from activation value. Rich event ingestion pays full freight even if unused.
  • Opaque ActionIQ — procurement-only; no public signal.

Recommendation

Vision: Price what TD does, not what it holds. Pay when TD delivers value — resolution, activation, outcomes — not when storage grows.
